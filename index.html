About

Where does the water go, and where does it wait?

I'm an M.S. student in the Department of Geosciences at Boise State University, working with Dr. Qifei Niu. My research asks how rain and snowmelt become streamflow, and how much water the subsurface holds back, across the contiguous U.S.

To get at this, I train Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models to simulate streamflow continuously across basins. A recurring theme in my work is snow: I feed snow information (UA-SWE) into the model to make it smarter where snow matters most.

I came to geosciences from hydrology and water resources engineering at the Southern University of Science and Technology (SUSTech), where I worked with Dr. Xiuyu Liang. There, I moved streamflow and nitrate time series from the time domain into the frequency domain, and studied how land cover shapes their variability in space and time. That path shaped how I like to work: I use the language of mathematics to describe nature quantitatively, then dig for the patterns and dominant controls underneath.

Outside the lab, you'll find me playing frisbee, listening to music, and traveling.

Research & projects

Snow, streams, and subsurface storage, studied with a mix of physics, statistics, and deep learning.

01 · Machine Learning

Snow-aware LSTM for ungauged basins

Snow stores a lot of the West's water, and most of its watersheds have no stream gauge. I couple a degree-day snowmelt module with an LSTM, train it on 531 CAMELS-US basins on an HPC cluster, and test how well it transfers to basins it has never seen.

In development

02 · Hydrologic Analysis

Dynamic subsurface water storage

Using Kirchner's recession theory, I estimate how much water the subsurface holds and how that storage shifts through time and space across the contiguous U.S., and what climate, terrain, and geology have to do with it.

In development

03 · Time Series Analysis

The fractal rhythm of streamflow and nitrate

For my B.S. thesis, I used multitaper spectral analysis to quantify how runoff and nitrate scale through time in 15 Iowa watersheds. Bigger, rounder, less densely drained watersheds acted as stronger filters, and land use shaped nitrate behavior.
